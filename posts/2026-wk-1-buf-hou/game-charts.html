Houston was the efficient team. On ordinary (non-explosive) plays it succeeded 38.7% of the time vs Buffalo's 20.5%. That is the steady way to move the ball: more snaps stay on schedule and drives run longer (Houston had 16 third downs to Buffalo's 9). More snaps are also more chances to give the ball away: both of the game's lost fumbles were Houston's.

Buffalo was the explosive team. Its 13 explosive plays were worth +27.7 EPA; everything else it did was worth -15.8. That style is volatile: it wins when the big plays land and looks broken when they don't. From Q2 00:26 to Q4 06:44 it ran 5 drives without a touchdown: 22 plays, 3 successes, 2 explosive plays, -4.1 EPA (1 end of half, 2 punts, 2 field goals). Houston had its own flat stretch: from Q4 10:31 to Q4 01:36 Houston ran 3 drives without a touchdown: 19 plays, 7 successes, 4 explosive plays, -3.9 EPA (1 field goal, 1 punt, 1 turnover).

It ended on defense. Explosiveness applies to defenses too: in the final two minutes Buffalo's defense made 2 sacks / takeaways worth +51 win-probability points.
